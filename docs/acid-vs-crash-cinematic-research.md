## Research contact (23 September 2026)

- [Iain Softley interview, Nerdist](https://nerdist.com/article/hackers-director-iain-softley-interview/):
  the director describes an architectural digital Manhattan, physically built,
  photographed with motion control and rich exposure. The useful principle is
  traversable luminous architecture with physical scale, not neon UI furniture.
- [Richard Morrison interview and film stills, Art of the Title](https://www.artofthetitle.com/title/hackers/):
  practical perspex structures, layered color work, and separately photographed
  title-color passes. Inspected the four-image tower contact sheet directly:
  repeated vertical data faces, dark corridors, bright horizontal rails,
  restrained blue depth, occasional warm contrast. These are observations of
  the published stills, not a claim to have watched the full movie in this run.
- [Archived production notes](https://ibiblio.org/team/history/hack/prod.html):
  the city/computer parallel and superposition of inside/outside support one
  coherent machine space. These are publicity provenance, not independent
  confirmation of every retrospective production recollection.

No film dialogue, image assets, or soundtrack is copied into the demo. The
sources inform original geometry and composition. The interviews share the
same production provenance; they are complementary accounts, not independent
proof of visual quality.

## Art direction decisions

Keep subsystem world anchors. Give them distinct silhouettes and illuminated
structural edges: gateway, switching prism, vault, terminal core, data stacks,
and a screen portal. Dark space between luminous structures is essential.
Use a lower camera for depth, targeted closeups for stakes, and only occasional
chromatic separation. A large permanent table of labels would defeat this.

The default is a shot-directed machine from the first frame. Quiet is dim and
architectural; action changes camera, field intensity, framing and physical
counter-effects. The input occupies three stable rows. Legacy flat/cyber
realizations remain explicit comparison tools. Presentation never changes the
battle reducer or story facts.

## Acceptance boundaries

Replay, finite cameras, terminal lifecycle, depth and frozen-frame invariants
are executable claims. Readability, variety, and composition require actual
frame inspection. An external viewer's "this must be an image protocol"
reaction remains unverified until that viewer supplies it. Screenshots from
PTY reconstruction approximate cell appearance; they are not an emulator-wide
font or perceptual certification.
