# v0.9 view transitions: `SurfaceFx::Offset` and `SurfaceFx::Blend`

Status: RED slice (amended for the NAV-1 freeze). The variants exist and `apply` is a no-op
stub; the tests in `tests/surface_fx_transitions.rs` and `tests/view_transition.rs` define the
contract and fail until it is implemented. The stubs `gibson::ui::ViewTransition`,
`Element::scoped_keys` and `surface_fx::resolve_reset` compile and are deliberately wrong.
Goal: continuous view transitions, "desktop-app feel, no jolts or flash".

## Signatures

```rust
pub enum SurfaceFx {
    // ... existing variants ...
    /// Shift the whole entity-local surface by (dx, dy) cells.
    Offset { dx: i32, dy: i32 },
    /// Move fg and bg of every painted cell toward `toward` by `t`.
    Blend { toward: Color, t: f32 },
}
```

Both are fully realized values like the other variants: no clock, no hidden state.

## Offset semantics

- The whole entity-local surface moves by `(dx, dy)`; positive is right/down.
- Cells moved outside the surface are clipped. Vacated cells become transparent (like
  `RowShift`), exposing the layer beneath.
- A wide glyph is moved or clipped as a whole. If its lead and continuation do not both land
  inside the surface it is dropped; no orphan continuation may appear on the edge.
- Style-only cells move like narrow cells. Transparent cells stay transparent.
- **Integer cells, horizontal, body-only (decision).** `Offset` moves whole cells and view
  transitions use `dy == 0` only. Apply it to a view's body, never to the root `screen`, the
  navigation rail or the prompt row: those stay fixed while the body slides, and a test pins
  a fixed header row and prompt row byte-identical at every progress value.
- `Offset { dx: 0, dy: 0 }` is exact identity. Chains stay ordered, never commuting by
  assumption (`Offset` then `Blend` equals `Blend` then `Offset` only because Blend is
  position-independent).
- Suggested implementation: a scratch `Surface::new_transparent` plus
  `blit_transparent_clipped` already clips wide glyphs whole. Caveat: blit overlays
  style-only cells onto the (transparent) destination instead of copying them, so copy
  cells directly for those. `shift_row` is the pattern for the per-row case.

## Blend semantics

- Applies to every painted (non-transparent) cell, including style-only cells and wide
  continuations (which share the lead's style). Transparent cells are untouched.
- `t` is clamped to [0, 1]; NaN is treated as 0. `t = 0` is identity, `t = 1` sets fg and bg
  exactly to `toward`.
- Per channel (fg and bg independently) with both endpoints resolvable (`Rgb`, `AnsiN`, or a
  named colour): `Color::lerp_resolved(toward, t)`, giving `Rgb` with per-channel rounding.
  Note `lerp_resolved` itself does not guard NaN; `Blend` must clamp first.
- Glyphs and attributes (bold, dim, italic, underline, reverse) are unchanged.

### Reset / unresolvable endpoint rule (proposed)

`Color::Reset` means "terminal default" and has no RGB value. `Color::lerp` would invent
`(204, 204, 204)`. Proposed rule: when the cell channel is `Reset` or unset (`None`), or
`toward` is `Reset`, use a step: keep the original for `t < 0.5`, take `toward` for
`t >= 0.5`. No fake gray is ever produced. The step is a visible pop for default-coloured
cells; an app that wants a smooth fade must give its cells explicit colours (this is the
documented consequence, and the tests pin it).

Unset (`None`) channels follow the same step rule, so a fade-out toward the background
colour does hide default-coloured cells at `t >= 0.5`. Open: whether `None` should instead
be left alone.

#### `resolve_reset`: removing the pop

`surface_fx::resolve_reset(surface, fg, bg)` replaces every `Some(Color::Reset)` fg/bg on a
painted cell with the given concrete colours (the skin's real foreground and background).
Run it once on a view's surface before its first `Blend`; the blend then lerps smoothly and
the step at `t = 0.5` never fires for those cells. It leaves transparent cells, unset (`None`)
channels (they inherit the layer beneath, which is not the terminal default), glyphs and
attributes alone, so `None` channels keep the step rule above. Currently a no-op stub.

### 256-colour and 16-colour behaviour

`Blend` always writes `Rgb`; downsampling is the renderer's job (`capability::quantize_color`,
`nearest256`). Along the grey axis (black toward white) nearest-palette selection is monotone,
so an 8-step sweep never moves away from the target, and `t = 1` lands exactly on the target
palette entry; the tests assert this. For arbitrary hues the xterm cube is non-uniform and
monotonicity of the quantized path is not guaranteed; the tests only assert it for the
unquantized path and exact arrival at `t = 1`. At `Ansi16` and `Mono` a fade is necessarily
coarse (16 steps of colour, or none). Apps should drive such fades with `Dim`/`Dissolve`
or snap. Open: ordered dithering across the sweep to hide palette stepping.

## How an app does a slide or a cross-fade

Exit ghosts (keeping a removed view alive for its exit animation) are deferred, so the app
keeps the outgoing view itself and drives a 30 fps `Tick`. `ViewTransition` (below) computes
the effect chains; the app owns the clock and both views:

1. On navigation, keep `outgoing` (its `Element`) alongside `incoming`; record `started`.
2. Each `Tick`, `let (out_fx, in_fx) = transition.effects(now, pref, width)`.
3. Slide: `outgoing.post_process(out_fx)` and `incoming.post_process(in_fx)`, stacked in one
   parent. Outgoing gets `dx = -round(p*W)`, incoming `dx = round((1-p)*W)` for
   `Dir::Left` (mirrored for `Dir::Right`). Vacated cells are transparent, so no blank flash.
4. Cross-fade: both views stacked; `Blend { toward: bg, t: p }` outgoing and
   `Blend { toward: bg, t: 1 - p }` incoming, so the seam passes through the surface colour
   (no true alpha exists; see `surface_fx.rs` module docs). Call `resolve_reset` on default
   coloured cells first, or accept the step pop.
5. When `is_settled`, drop `outgoing`: both chains are empty and the settled tree is the plain
   incoming view, byte-identical to one that was never animated.

Wiring: `Element::post_process` already exists (`src/ui/element.rs`) and is applied at
lowering (`src/ui/compile.rs`) after semantic motion, for every element kind except `Modal`
and `Toast`, which skip effects. `Node::post_process` (`src/painter.rs`) is the lower-level
equivalent. There is no open ask for an element-level hook; the ui `.motion` / `MotionPlan`
path still emits only fixed role grammars and does not carry `Offset`/`Blend`. Differential
rendering already writes only changed cells: a slide of a mostly static backdrop costs the
moving content plus the vacated edge, never a full clear.

## `ViewTransition` (RED stub)

```rust
#[non_exhaustive] pub struct ViewTransition { pub started: Duration, pub duration: Duration, pub kind: TransitionKind }
#[non_exhaustive] pub enum TransitionKind { Slide(Dir), Fade { toward: Color } }
#[non_exhaustive] pub enum Dir { Left, Right }

ViewTransition::new(started, duration, kind) -> Self
progress(&self, now, pref: MotionPreference) -> f32          // eased 0..=1
is_settled(&self, now, pref) -> bool
accepts_pointer(&self, now, pref) -> bool
effects(&self, now, pref, width: u16) -> (Vec<SurfaceFx>, Vec<SurfaceFx>) // (outgoing, incoming)
```

- Easing is ease-in-out. Progress is 0 before `started` and exactly 1 at and after the deadline.
- `MotionPreference::None`: progress 1 immediately, settled, both chains empty.
- `MotionPreference::Reduced`: whatever the kind, the transition becomes a `Blend`-only fade
  capped at 80 ms (the same cap as `MotionTokens::resolve`, `src/ui/motion.rs`). Reduced never
  slides and never emits `Offset`.
- `Slide` uses one `Offset` per side with `dy == 0` and `dx` of opposite signs; `Fade` uses one
  `Blend` per side toward the same colour.
- Exported from `gibson::ui` and `gibson::ui::prelude`.

## Outgoing-view contract

The app keeps the outgoing `Element` alive for the duration, and:

1. namespaces its keys with `.scoped_keys("out")`: duplicate explicit keys are a hard error
   (`UiError::DuplicateKey`), so two copies of a view cannot otherwise share a tree.
   `Element::scoped_keys(prefix)` prefixes every explicit key in the subtree, overlays included,
   as `"{prefix}.{key}"`; unkeyed elements keep their position-derived `Key::Path`, already unique;
2. marks it `.disabled(true)`. `disabled` is inherited by every descendant at lowering, so the
   outgoing controls leave the Tab ring and are not pointer targets.

The outgoing view is therefore visible but inert. Keys on the incoming view stay live throughout.

## Overlays under a transition

Modal and toast elements are not post-processed (effects are skipped for them at lowering) and
overlays paint after the base. So with `screen().child(view.post_process(fx)).overlay(modal(..))`
the modal's and the toast's cells are identical at every progress value: unmoved and unblended,
while the body moves or fades beneath them. A test pins both halves (overlay unchanged, body
did change).

## Pointer input during a transition (decision)

Hit-testing uses the pre-effect extent whenever any fx applies (`src/ui/hit.rs`), so during a
slide pointer targets lag the pixels. Decision: **freeze pointer input while a transition is not
settled; keys stay live.** `ViewTransition::accepts_pointer(now, pref)` is false until settled and
true after (always true under `MotionPreference::None`); the app drops mouse events while it is
false. Currently a stub returning `true`.

## Read-only rows

A read-only row that explains itself is a focusable `button(..).on_press(ShowHint)`, not
`.disabled(true)`: a disabled control leaves the Tab ring, so a keyboard user could never reach
the hint. Use `.disabled` only for inert, genuinely unavailable controls (for example the
outgoing view of a transition).

## Alt+M under a modal (contract)

While a modal is active, `UiRuntime::handle_event` consumes every `Key` and `Paste` event
(`src/ui/runtime.rs`), Alt chords included. Global hotkeys such as Alt+M must therefore be
routed by the app **before** calling `UiRuntime::handle_event`. A `set_global_keys` API is
deferred.

## Sprite-present testing rule (owner rule)

Every transition test must also run with a truecolor sprite region on the surface. The test
fixture is synthetic: a 12x6 block of `\u{2580}` half blocks, distinct `Rgb` fg and bg per
cell from a deterministic formula defined in the test file. No avatar or image asset is used
or read. `Offset` must move the sprite intact (glyph, fg, bg of every cell preserved at the
shifted position, nothing outside the clip); `Blend` at `t` must apply the exact per-cell
lerp to the sprite's own colours. The renderer test slides a full view containing the sprite
through 8 frames: no frame after the first contains `\x1b[2J` (a fullscreen first frame may
clear to anchor), each later frame writes fewer bytes than the first full paint, and the
presented cells carry the sprite at the shifted position.

## Breaking change

Adding variants to the public `SurfaceFx` enum breaks downstream exhaustive `match`es.
Acceptable for 0.9.0.

- **Release-owner ruling:** `SurfaceFx` gets `#[non_exhaustive]` in 0.9.0, so later variants are
  not breaking. Downstream `match` sites need one wildcard arm.
  The only crate-internal exhaustive match is `SurfaceFx::apply` in `src/surface_fx.rs`.
  `#[non_exhaustive]` does not affect in-crate matches.
- **C ABI and bindings:** `SurfaceFx` does not cross them. It has no occurrence in
  `include/`, `abi/` or `bindings/`, so this change does not touch the ABI version.
- **Consumer pre-check:** Minerva (ProjectGHOST main `4171b3d`, 253 `.rs` files, 60 of
  them using libgibson) has 0 references to `SurfaceFx`, so 0 exhaustive matches. The
  0.9.0 consumer gate will confirm this.
- **Other public enums 0.9.0 may grow:**
  - `ui::ControlRole` (`src/ui/skin.rs`): exhaustive, and custom skins match on it. If
    `toggle` gets its own role, add `#[non_exhaustive]` with it.
  - `Event` and `MouseKind` (`src/input.rs`): already `#[non_exhaustive]`, so pointer
    routing needs no change there.
  - `ElementKind` is `pub(crate)` and does not apply.
  - No blanket change.

## Open questions for NAV-1

1. Decided: `Offset` stays integer cells, horizontal for view transitions, body-only.
   Fractional/sub-cell motion (half-block vertical) is not in 0.9.
2. Where do transitions live? Decided for 0.9: element-level `Element::post_process` (it
   exists) driven by the app through `ViewTransition`; the app owns the outgoing view (see the
   outgoing-view contract). Open: a `MotionPlan` role (`Navigate`?), and who owns the outgoing
   view's lifetime once exit ghosts land.
3. Reset/`None` step rule: kept, plus the opt-in `resolve_reset` helper for `Reset` channels
   (`None` keeps the step rule). Resolving at lowering time stays open.
4. Easing and duration live in the app. Provide helpers (ease-in-out, 30 fps clamp) in
   `gibson::ui`, or keep fx values dumb?
5. Decided: `Reduced` maps to a `Blend`-only fade capped at 80 ms, never a slide; `None` is an
   instant switch.
6. `Blend` toward `Reset` (the terminal default): step to `Reset` at `t >= 0.5`; is a
   fade-to-default ever wanted, or should it be rejected?
7. Decided: freeze pointer input while a transition is not settled; keys stay live
   (`ViewTransition::accepts_pointer`).
8. Should `Blend` also cover cells whose colour comes from the terminal's underlying
   layer (transparent cells)? This slice says no: transparent stays transparent.
